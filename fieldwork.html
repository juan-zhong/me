AYHAK · 安远话记录计划

Anyuan Hakka Documentation Project

Documenting, studying, and keeping alive the Hakka spoken in Anyuan County, Jiangxi, together with its speakers

Anyuan Hakka (安远话) is a lesser-studied variety of Hakka spoken in Anyuan County, southern Jiangxi. As more young people move away and grow up speaking Mandarin, everyday use of the language is shrinking. AYHAK is a community-based project, led by a native speaker, that records the language, analyzes it, and turns what we learn into resources the community can use.

Why AYHAK

Hakka is often described through a few well-studied varieties, while the speech of individual counties like Anyuan remains largely undocumented. Anyuan Hakka also differs from town to town and village to village. AYHAK aims to:

  • describe the sound system and grammar of Anyuan Hakka;
  • record vocabulary tied to local life, such as plants, food, farming, and the market;
  • give speakers, at home and away, ways to keep using the language and to learn about it.

How we work

  • With speakers, not just about them. Community members take part as collaborators, consultants, and learners.
  • There is no single "standard" Anyuan Hakka. We record variation across towns and villages instead of choosing one form as correct.
  • Results go back to the community. Courses, meetups, and the photo book are made for Anyuan people first, and in their own language where possible.
  • Consent comes first. Recordings and personal information are shared only with speakers' permission.

Join a meetup 安远话复健会

Our online meetups are a place for people from Anyuan, wherever they live now, to keep speaking Anyuan Hakka. We meet every other Friday evening (Beijing time) on Tencent Meeting: half free conversation, half a chosen topic. Participants add their town or village to their display name, so everyone can hear how the language varies.

Questions

Is Anyuan Hakka a language or a dialect?
It is commonly called a dialect (方言) in China. Linguistically, it is a variety of Hakka with its own sounds, words, and grammar, and it deserves documentation in its own right.
I'm from Anyuan. How can I take part?
Join a meetup, share words or stories from your town or village, or help with recordings. Any level of fluency is welcome.
I don't speak Anyuan Hakka. Can I still join the course or meetups?
Yes. The upcoming course is taught in Mandarin and open to non-speakers, and listeners are welcome at the meetups.
I'm a researcher. Can I use the data?
Recordings are shared only with speakers' consent. Please get in touch to discuss collaboration or access.
