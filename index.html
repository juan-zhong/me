Study the margins. Rethink the norms. Create space together.

I am a fourth-year PhD student in linguistics at University at Buffalo, where I am advised by Jean-Pierre Koenig and affiliated with UB GramLab and the UB fieldwork group. My research spans morphosyntax, discourse pragmatics, and language documentation, with fieldwork experience in Hakka, Balti, Bimoba, Quechua, and Aymara. I hold a BA from Beijing Foreign Studies University and an MA from Columbia University.

I'm drawn to what falls outside the norm: linguistic phenomena that challenge existing theories, language varieties marginalized by standardization, and people who question the norms imposed on them. I study them as a lens for rethinking the norms themselves, and I work with the communities involved to create more space for them, through research, teaching, and community projects.

Research

My research looks at language from three angles: what grammar does at the edges of our theories, how under-resourced languages can be documented and sustained, and whose language experience is represented in research.

01 Theoratical Linguistics

Affective meaning and the grammar of emotion

How does grammar encode emotion, and how are emotional expressions shaped by the speakers' relationship, experiences, and knowledge? I study affective verbal suffixes in Quechua and Aymara, such as -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Unlocking evaluative verb morphology in a Central Andean Oral Story. Paper presented at Unlocking Evaluative Morphology II: Semantic values and pragmatic motivations, 20–22 April 2026, Alicante, Spain. [abstract]

Morphosyntax/semantics interface and the grammar of dish names

How do languages package complex concepts into single word-like expressions? My qualifying paper analyzes Mandarin dish names and discusses what it informs us about the architecture of language as a specalized-domain grammar.

Zhong, Juan & Jean-Pierre Koenig. 2026. The grammar of dish names in Mandarin Chinese. Paper presented at the 33rd International Conference on Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar, 3–4 August 2026, Bergen, Norway. [abstract]

Information structure and focus constructions

How do interlocutors manage each other's attention in conversation? I have explored focus constructions in Mandarin, Hakka, Cantonese, Gan, Xiang, Min, Wu, Jin, Yucatec Maya, and Bimoba.

02 Language Documentation & Resources

Anyuan Hakka (AYHAK)

Anyuan Hakka is my native language. Since 2021 I have led a community-based project to document, analyze, and revitalize it, including an IPA illustration, an edible-plant photo book with vernacular names, IPA, and audio, and introductory linguistics courses for community members. See AYHAK for details.

03 Language, Identity & Access

Migrant Children and Language Education

In Beijing, I designed after-school language programs for children of migrant workers and worked as a research assistant on the 2019 Blue Book of Migrant Children Education in China (21st Century Education Research Institute).

Zhong, Juan. 2019. A task-based program for teaching English to children of migrant workers in Beijing. Paper presented at the 8th International Conference on Task-Based Language Teaching, 19–21 August 2019, Ottawa, Canada.

Chinese Queer Corpus (CQC)

I co-develop the Chinese Queer Corpus, a Mandarin speech corpus of speakers who self-identify as diverse gender or sexualities. Responsible data governance is part of its design: researchers access the data through a data use agreement, balancing open research with the protection of speakers.
