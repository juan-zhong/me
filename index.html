I am a fourth-year PhD student in linguistics at University at Buffalo, where I am advised by Jean-Pierre Koenig and affiliated with UB GramLab and the UB fieldwork group. My research spans morphosyntax, discourse pragmatics, and indigenous language documentation, with fieldwork experience in Mandarin, Hakka, Balti, Bimoba, Quechua, and Aymara. I hold a BA from Beijing Foreign Studies University and an MA from Columbia University.

I'm drawn to what falls outside the norm: linguistic phenomena that challenge existing theories, language varieties marginalized by standardization, and people who question the norms imposed on them. I study them not as exceptions but as a lens for rethinking the norms themselves, and I work with the communities involved to make more room for them, through research, teaching, and community projects.

Research

Language and Emotion

How does grammar encode emotion cross-linguistically? How are the linguistic expressions of emotion constrained by the interlocutors' relationship, experiences, and knowledge? Currently I'm working on the affective suffixes attested in Quechua and Aymara varieties.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Unlocking evaluative verb morphology in a Central Andean Oral Story. Paper presented at Unlocking Evaluative Morphology II: Semantic values and pragmatic motivations, 20–22 April 2026, Alicante, Spain. [abstract]

The Grammar of Dish Names

How do we categorize complex concepts linguistically? Complex compounds provide a lens for understanding the interface of morphology/syntax, and lexicon/grammar. My qualifying paper investigates the structure and meaning of complex compound nouns using a Mandarin dish name dataset.

Zhong, Juan & Jean-Pierre Koenig. 2026. The grammar of dish names in Mandarin Chinese. Paper presented at the 33rd International Conference on Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar, 3–4 August 2026, Bergen, Norway. [abstract]

Case and Argument Realization

When can a grammatical marker stray from its prototypical job? In South Bolivian Quechua, locative arguments can carry either spatial case or the accusative -ta. I investigate the semantic and discourse constraints that license accusative marking on locatives.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Explaining accusative case marking on locative arguments in South Bolivian Quechua. Paper presented at the 2026 Annual Meeting of the Linguistic Society of America, 9 January 2026. [abstract] [slides]

Language Documentation

Since 2021, I have initiated an ongoing Hakka documentation project (AYHAK), in which I collaborate with community members to document, analyze, preserve, and promote the language. Current work includes an edible-plant photo book with vernacular names, IPA, and audio, and an introductory course on Anyuan Hakka linguistics. See the AYHAK page for details.

Zhong, Juan. 2026. Anyuan Hakka documentation project. Paper presented at 4to Coloquio Internacional de Lingüística en Bolivia [Presentation in Anyuan Hakka], 20–21 March 2026, La Paz, Bolivia. [abstract]

Language, Gender, and Sexuality

I co-develop the Chinese Queer Corpus (CQC), a Mandarin speech corpus of gender and sexual minority speakers. The corpus is available to researchers through a data use agreement, balancing open research with the protection of speakers.

Information Structure

How do interlocutors manage each other's attention in conversation? To address this question, I've explored focus constructions in different Chinese dialects, Yucatec Maya, and Bimoba.
