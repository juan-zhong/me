Research

I study linguistics mainly from three angles: what grammar does at the edges of existing theories, how under-resourced languages can be documented and sustained, and whose language experience is represented in research. The projects below are connected, but each also stands on its own.

01 Theoretical Linguistics

Affective Meaning and the Grammar of Emotion

How does grammar encode emotion, and how are affective expressions shaped by the speakers' relations, experiences, and knowledge? I study affective verbal suffixes in Andean languages, currently focusing on Quechua -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Unlocking evaluative verb morphology in a Central Andean oral story. Paper presented at Unlocking Evaluative Morphology II: Semantic values and pragmatic motivations, 20–22 April 2026, Alicante, Spain. Abstract

Camacho-Rios, Gladys & Juan Zhong. In preparation. Affective verbal suffix -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

Morphosyntax/Semantics Interface and Dish Names

How do languages package complex concepts into single word-like expressions? My qualifying paper examines the structure and meaning of Mandarin dish names, treating them as a specialized-domain grammar and asking what they reveal about the general architecture of human language.

Zhong, Juan & Jean-Pierre Koenig. 2026. The grammar of dish names in Mandarin Chinese. Paper presented at The 33rd International Conference on Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar, 3–4 August 2026, Bergen, Norway. Abstract

Information Structure and Focus Constructions

How do interlocutors manage each other's attention in conversation? I have explored focus constructions in Mandarin, Hakka, Cantonese, Gan, Xiang, Min, Wu, Jin, Yucatec Maya, and Bimoba.

03 Language, Identity & Access

Chinese Queer Corpus

I co-develop the Chinese Queer Corpus, a Mandarin speech corpus of speakers who self-identify as having diverse sexes, genders, and sexualities. Responsible data governance is part of its design: researchers access the data through a data use agreement, balancing open research with the protection of speakers.

Migrant Children and Language Education

In Beijing, I designed after-school language programs for children of migrant workers and worked as a research assistant on the 2019 Blue Book of Migrant Children Education in China (21st Century Education Research Institute).

ACTTAn inclusive multilingual project

Arsuña CeTee Todos (ACTT) is a grassroots, multilingual initiative dedicated to creating an inclusive space for multicultural exchange through lived speech practices. Its name reflects its ethos: Arsuña (‘to speak’ in Aymara), CeTee (‘casual chat’ in Anyuan Hakka), and Todos (‘everyone’ in Spanish), foregrounding communication, informality, and openness across linguistic worlds.

ACTT is distinguished by its commitment to centering minoritized and community-based languages alongside global ones, and by its speaker-led, rotating format, in which linguistic authority is shared and reconfigured across sessions. Rather than adopting a classroom model, ACTT prioritizes interaction, everyday language use, and relational exchange, fostering a micro-community grounded in participation rather than instruction.

Through biweekly meetings structured around shared conversation, language-focused engagement, and open interaction, ACTT cultivates a space where multilingualism is not only practiced but experienced as a dynamic, collaborative process. By connecting speakers across different linguistic, cultural, and geographic contexts, the initiative seeks to reimagine language exchange as equitable, dialogic, and community-driven.

CTHHA community program for young people from under-resourced counties in China

In 2020, I collaborated with peers from Anyuan to establish a community-based program dedicated to supporting young people from under-resourced counties in China. The program aims to foster a supportive peer network where members share experiences, exchange perspectives, and empower one another through mutual learning and mentorship. We operate two main initiatives: the Mentoring for Growth (CTHH) plan, held twice annually during summer and winter breaks, and the Drop-in Chat Room (OHOH), held weekly during the fall semester.

In CTHH, people voluntarily sign up as mentors or mentees and are matched in pairs based on shared backgrounds and interests. Each pair engages in a 60–90 minute online conversation, during which mentors share personal stories and advice, while mentees bring questions about study, work, or life.

In OHOH, members from the CTHH plan freely join in the weekly online chatting room hosted by regular mentors and have conversations about their questions and interests in an informal, supportive environment.

As of 2026, we have completed 13 rounds of CTHH (2020–2026) and four rounds of OHOH (2022–2026). Additionally, we organized three special annual gatherings (2022, 2023, and 2026) featuring thematic discussions and invited guest talks that strengthened community engagement.
