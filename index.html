Study the margins. Rethink the norms. Create space together.

I am a fourth-year PhD student in linguistics at the University at Buffalo, advised by Jean-Pierre Koenig and affiliated with UB GramLab and the UB fieldwork group. I work on morphosyntax, discourse pragmatics, and language documentation, with fieldwork in Hakka, Balti, Bimoba, Quechua, and Aymara.

I'm drawn to what falls outside the norm: linguistic phenomena that challenge existing theories, language varieties marginalized by standardization, and people who question the norms imposed on them. Across research, teaching, and community collaboration, I question established assumptions and help create space for underrepresented languages, perspectives, and experiences.

My full record of publications, presentations, teaching, and service is in my CV.

Research

I look at language from three angles: what grammar does at the edges of our theories, how under-resourced languages can be documented and sustained, and whose language experience is represented in research. The projects below are connected, but each also stands on its own.

01 Linguistic Theory

Affective meaning and the grammar of emotion

How does grammar encode emotion, and how do interlocutors' relationships, experiences, and shared knowledge shape emotional expression? I study affective verbal suffixes in Andean languages, currently focusing on Quechua -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

PresentationZhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Unlocking evaluative verb morphology in a Central Andean oral story. Unlocking Evaluative Morphology II, Alicante, Spain. Abstract

In preparationCamacho-Rios, Gladys & Juan Zhong. Affective verbal suffix -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

Complex expressions and the grammar of dish names

How do languages package complex concepts into single word-like expressions? My qualifying paper examines the structure and meaning of Mandarin dish names, treating them as a specialized-domain grammar and asking what they reveal about the architecture of grammar.

PresentationZhong, Juan & Jean-Pierre Koenig. 2026. The grammar of dish names in Mandarin Chinese. 33rd International Conference on Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar, Bergen, Norway. Abstract

Information structure and focus

How do speakers direct and coordinate attention in discourse? I have explored focus constructions in Mandarin, Hakka, Cantonese, Gan, Xiang, Min, Wu, Jin, Yucatec Maya, and Bimoba.

02 Language Documentation

Anyuan Hakka

Anyuan Hakka is my native language. Since 2021, I have led AYHAK, a community-based project documenting the language and supporting its continued use. My linguistic work includes a phonetic description of the language, in preparation as an IPA illustration.

In preparationZhong, Juan, T. Yang & Matthew Faytak. Anyuan Hakka: An IPA illustration.

PresentationZhong, Juan. 2026. Anyuan Hakka documentation project. 4to Coloquio Internacional de Lingüística en Bolivia, La Paz, Bolivia. [Presented in Anyuan Hakka] Abstract

Quechua and Aymara

I work with oral narratives and fieldwork data from Quechua and Aymara varieties in Bolivia, contributing to collaborative research on affect and storytelling in Andean languages led by Gladys Camacho-Rios. My own questions here include when locative arguments take accusative case and how Quechua varieties form nominal compounds.

PublishedZhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Explaining accusative case marking on locative arguments in South Bolivian Quechua. Proceedings of the Linguistic Society of America 11(1): 6105. Paper (DOI) Slides

Invited talkZhong, Juan. 2025. Nominal compounding: A comparative analysis of South Bolivian Quechua and Tarma Quechua. UMSA, La Paz, Bolivia.

PresentationAkobundu, U., J. Baiz, G. Camacho-Rios, X. Lin, N. Silvestre-Cruz, S. Spies, G. Townes & J. Zhong. 2025. Complex consonant clusters in the morphotactics of Southern Bolivian Aymara verbs. PHUNY 2, Buffalo.

Balti

A systematic overview of quantification in Balti, a western Tibetic language, based on elicitation with a native speaker.

Course paperZhong, Juan. 2021. Quantifiers in Balti. Language Documentation & Field Methods, Columbia University. Available upon request.

03 Language, Identity & Access

Chinese Queer Corpus (CQC)

I co-develop the Chinese Queer Corpus, a Mandarin speech corpus of speakers who self-identify as diverse gender or sexualities. Responsible data governance is part of its design: researchers access the data through a data use agreement, balancing open research with the protection of speakers.

Migrant children and language education

In Beijing, I designed after-school language programs for children of migrant workers and worked as a research assistant on the 2019 Blue Book of Migrant Children Education in China (21st Century Education Research Institute).

PresentationZhong, Juan. 2019. A task-based program for teaching English to children of migrant workers in Beijing. 8th International Conference on Task-Based Language Teaching, Ottawa, Canada.

Projects

Alongside research, I build things with and for communities.

AYHAK · Anyuan Hakka Documentation Project

A community project for my hometown language: biweekly online meetups where people from Anyuan keep speaking Anyuan Hakka, an edible-plant photo book with local names and audio (in development), and introductory linguistics courses taught in Anyuan Hakka, with a Mandarin-language course coming soon.

CTHH · Mentoring for young people from under-resourced counties

A peer mentoring program I co-founded in 2020 with peers from Anyuan, matching mentors and mentees from under-resourced counties in China. Thirteen rounds so far.

ACTT · Arsuña CeTee Todos

A speaker-led, biweekly multilingual exchange that places minoritized languages such as Aymara and Anyuan Hakka alongside global ones.
