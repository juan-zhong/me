I'm a third-year PhD student in linguistics at University at Buffalo, advised by Jean-Pierre Koenig. I'm also a member of UB GramLab and UB fieldwork group. My research interests center around morphosyntax, discourse pragmatics, and indigenous language documentation. Languages I have worked on include Mandarin, Hakka, Balti, Bimoba, Quechua, and Aymara. Prior to joining UB, I received my BA from Beijing Foreign Studies University and my MA from Columbia University. Here is my CV.

Research

Language and Emotion

How does grammar encode emotion cross-linguistically? How are the linguistic expressions of emotion constrained by the interlocutors' relationship, experiences, and knowledge? Currently I'm working on the affective/evaluative/expressive suffixes attested in Quechua and Aymara varieties.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Unlocking evaluative verb morphology in a Central Andean Oral Story. Paper presented at Unlocking Evaluative Morphology II: Semantic values and pragmatic motivations, 20-22 April 2026, Alicante, Spain. [abstract]

Grammar of dish names

How do we categorize complex concepts linguistically? Complex compounds provide a lens for understanding the interface of morphology/syntax, and lexcon/grammar. My qualifying paper investigates the structure and meaning of complex compound nouns using a Mandarin dish name dataset.

Zhong, Juan & Jean-Pierre Koenig. 2026. The grammar of dish names in Mandarin Chinese. Paper to be presented at the 33rd International Conference on Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar, 3-4 August, 2026, Bergen, Norway. [abstract]

Information Structure

How do interlocutors manage each other's attention in conversation? To address this question, I've explored focus constructions in different Chinese dialects, Yucatec Maya, and Bimoba.

Language Documentation

Since 2021, I have initiated an ongoing Hakka documentation project (AYHAK), in which I collaborate with community members to document, analyze, preserve, and promote the language. Click here for more details about my AYHAK project.
