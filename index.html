Research

I study linguistics mainly from three angles: what grammar does at the edges of existing theories, how under-resourced languages can be documented and sustained, and whose language experience is represented in research. The projects below are connected, but each also stands on its own.

01 Theoretical Linguistics

Affective Meaning & the Grammar of Emotion

How does grammar encode emotion, and how are affective expressions shaped by the speakers' relations, experiences, and knowledge? I study affective verbal suffixes in Andean languages, currently focusing on Quechua -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Unlocking evaluative verb morphology in a Central Andean oral story. Paper presented at Unlocking Evaluative Morphology II: Semantic values and pragmatic motivations, 20–22 April 2026, Alicante, Spain. Abstract

Camacho-Rios, Gladys & Juan Zhong. In preparation. Affective verbal suffix -ri in Central Andean oral stories.

Morphosyntax/Semantics Interface & Dish Names

How do languages package complex concepts into single word-like expressions? My qualifying paper examines the structure and meaning of Mandarin dish names, treating them as a specialized-domain grammar and asking what they reveal about the general architecture of human language.

Zhong, Juan & Jean-Pierre Koenig. 2026. The grammar of dish names in Mandarin Chinese. Paper presented at The 33rd International Conference on Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar, 3–4 August 2026, Bergen, Norway. Abstract

Information Structure & Focus Constructions

How do interlocutors manage each other's attention in conversation? I have explored focus constructions in Mandarin, Hakka, Cantonese, Gan, Xiang, Min, Wu, Jin, Yucatec Maya, and Bimoba.

02 Language Documentation & Resources

Hakka

Anyuan Hakka is my native language. Since 2021, I have led AYHAK, a community-based project to document, analyze, and revitalize it.

Zhong, Juan. 2026. Anyuan Hakka documentation project. Paper presented at 4to Coloquio Internacional de Lingüística en Bolivia, 20–21 March 2026, La Paz, Bolivia. [Presented in Anyuan Hakka] Abstract

Quechua & Aymara

I work with Dr. Gladys Camacho-Rios on fieldwork data (oral stories and naturalistic conversation) from lesser-studied varieties of Quechua and Aymara in Bolivia, focusing on affective and quotative forms. Other questions I have looked into include argument alternation, nominal compounding, and complex consonant clusters.

Zhong, Juan & Gladys Camacho-Rios. 2026. Explaining accusative case marking on locative arguments in South Bolivian Quechua. Proceedings of the Linguistic Society of America 11(1): 6105. Paper (DOI) Slides

Zhong, Juan. 2025. Nominal compounding: A comparative analysis of South Bolivian Quechua and Tarma Quechua. Invited talk, Sociedad Científica de Estudiantes de la Carrera de Lingüística e Idiomas, UMSA, 10 January 2025, La Paz, Bolivia. Slides

Akobundu, U., J. Baiz, G. Camacho-Rios, X. Lin, N. Silvestre-Cruz, S. Spies, G. Townes & J. Zhong. 2025. Complex consonant clusters in the morphotactics of Southern Bolivian Aymara verbs. Paper presented at Second Annual Meeting of Phonetics/Phonology in Upstate New York (PHUNY 2), 3 May 2025, Buffalo, United States.

Balti

A systematic overview of quantification in Balti, a western Tibetic language, based on elicitation with a native speaker.

Zhong, Juan. 2021. Quantifiers in Balti. Unpublished course paper, Language Documentation & Field Methods, Columbia University. Available upon request.

03 Language, Identity & Access

Chinese Queer Corpus

I co-develop the Chinese Queer Corpus, a Mandarin speech corpus of speakers who self-identify as having diverse sexes, genders, and sexualities. Responsible data governance is part of its design: researchers access the data through a data use agreement, balancing open research with the protection of speakers.

Migrant Children & Language Education

In Beijing, I designed after-school language programs for children of migrant workers and worked as a research assistant on the 2019 Blue Book of Migrant Children Education in China (21st Century Education Research Institute).

Zhong, Juan. 2019. A task-based program for teaching English to children of migrant workers in Beijing. Paper presented at The 8th International Conference on Task-Based Language Teaching, 19–21 August 2019, Ottawa, Canada.

Old Chinese & Historiography

My historiographic work as a linguist and as a translator includes analyzing old Chinese texts from excavated bamboo-slip manuscripts and translating Gu Jiegang's 1923 letter that launched the "layered formation of ancient Chinese history" thesis.

Community

AYHAKAnyuan Hakka Documentation Project

A community project for Anyuan Hakka, my hometown language (since 2021): an online dictionary and 500 Everyday Sentences with recordings, biweekly online meetups, a plant photo book (in progress), introductory linguistics courses taught in Anyuan Hakka (2024), and Speaking Anyuan Hakka, a Hakka language course taught in Mandarin (coming soon).

AYHAK website

ACTTAn inclusive multilingual project

Arsuña CeTee Todos (ACTT) is a grassroots, speaker-led language exchange. Its name joins Aymara arsuña ‘to speak’, Anyuan Hakka CeTee ‘casual chat’, and Spanish todos ‘everyone’. In biweekly meetings, speakers take turns leading the conversation, with community languages alongside global ones.

ACTT 2026 calendar

CTHHA community program for young people from under-resourced counties in China

A peer-support network I co-founded with peers from Anyuan in 2020. It runs Mentoring for Growth (CTHH), one-on-one online mentoring held each summer and winter break, and the Drop-in Chat Room (OHOH), held weekly during the fall semester. As of 2026: 13 rounds of CTHH, four rounds of OHOH, and three annual gatherings.
